What connects my work

Understanding the whole measurement.

Astronomy first drew me to questions about signals that are difficult to observe. My graduate work brought me closer to the device itself: how coupled optical resonators shape the frequencies and modes available to a photonic system.

Work on an outdoor photon-counting link emphasized the experimental side—alignment, calibration, background, and the conditions under which a result is measured. My more recent detector-waveform study follows the next step: how the information in a signal survives preprocessing, modeling, and finite-precision hardware.

I want to develop instruments by thinking about these stages together. I am especially interested in research where physical models can be tested, numerical results can be traced, and implementation choices are judged against the needs of the measurement.

Teaching & engineering practice

Making complex ideas testable.

At Northeastern, I supported electrical engineering laboratory and electromagnetics instruction. I also teach mathematics and engineering through worked examples and structured problem sets.

My engineering work includes product setup, functional validation, and translating field issues into reproducible tests. Building software such as UnionGPT has also shaped how I think about source material, traceable outputs, and tools that people can inspect.
